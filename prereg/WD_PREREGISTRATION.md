# Stage 5 Pre-Registration: Pushing on the Weight-Decay Law

Written and committed **before any Stage 5 run existed**. The predictions in
[`wd_predictions.json`](wd_predictions.json) were generated by
`python -m grokking.src.wd_analysis --freeze-predictions`, which refuses to overwrite them, and the
analysis script reads them from that file. The git history is the timestamp: this file and the
predictions arrive in the same commit as the experiment code, before any file under `runs/wd_push/`.
Anything changed after that commit is listed under *Deviations* below.

## Why

Stage 4B found grokking time ∝ `wd^β` with β = −0.964, 95% CI [−1.049, −0.879] (seed fixed effects,
`src/robustness.py`). That matches a known result: Lyu et al. (2024) prove $t_g = \frac{1}{\lambda} \log \alpha$
for homogeneous networks with large initialisation scale α and small weight decay λ. Matching the
1/λ shape alone does not distinguish this theory from other explanations. These experiments test the
parts that do, and they test whether the weight norm, a signal that needs no reverse engineering,
forecasts grokking for individual seeds.

## Fixed settings

- Task, model and optimiser as in Stages 2–4: (a + b) mod 113, 30% training split, one-layer
  transformer, full-batch AdamW, β = (0.9, 0.98). One epoch is one optimiser step.
- Seeds 0–4 throughout. Metrics logged every 100 epochs.
- **Grokking time**: first logged epoch with test accuracy ≥ 0.95. A run that never reaches it is
  *censored* at its budget: reported as not grokked, never imputed.
- Runs: `python -m grokking.src.wd_experiments`. Analysis: `python -m grokking.src.wd_analysis`.

## Hypotheses and decision rules

**H0 — sanity (gate).** At lr 1e-3, wd 1.0, α = 1, init = split = seed, each seed's grokking time
lies in (old − 500, old], where *old* is its Stage 4B value logged every 500 epochs.
If any seed fails, stop and find out why before interpreting H1–H5.

**H1 — the clock is lr × wd (E2).** AdamW shrinks every weight by a factor (1 − lr·wd) per step, so if
the weight-decay phase is the bottleneck, grokking time should depend on the product lr·wd only.
Fit log t = a_seed + b_lr·log lr + b_wd·log wd.
*Supported* if both 95% CIs include −1. *Rejected* for lr if the b_lr CI excludes −1.
Also reported: grokking times in the three cells with lr·wd = 1e-3, and the median per-seed
forecast error |log(observed / predicted)| against the table below.

**H2 — grokking time rises with log α (E1).** Fit t = a_seed + slope·ln α.
*Direction supported* if the slope's 95% CI lies above 0.
*Quantitatively consistent* with Lyu et al.'s leading order if the CI includes
1000 epochs per unit ln α (= 1 / (lr·wd)).
Caveat fixed now: α = 0.5 lies outside the large-initialisation regime the theory covers.

**H3 — 1/wd extrapolates (E3).** Per-seed predictions below.
*Supported* if at least 80% of the 30 runs are correct: grokked within a factor of 1.5 of the
prediction, or did not grok where the prediction exceeds the 120k budget (including all wd = 0 runs).
Where the law breaks at large wd, that is reported as its edge, not explained away.

**H4 — initialisation vs split (E4). Exploratory, no directional prediction.** Two-way decomposition
of log grokking time over the 5 × 5 grid (F-tests with the interaction as error, df 4 and 16), and
key-frequency Jaccard overlap for pairs sharing only the init, only the split, or neither.

**H5 — weight-norm alarm (all runs).** Alarm: first logged epoch where weight_l2_norm <= 0.90 * running max.
Expected from Stage 4B: it fires before grokking in nearly every grokked run but mainly reflects the
configuration, not the seed. Fit log t = a_config + slope·log t_alarm.
*Supported* if the slope's 95% CI excludes 1. Also reported: false alarms (alarm fired, no grokking
within budget) and misses (grokked with no alarm).

## Known limitations, stated in advance

- Five seeds per cell; one task, one architecture, one training fraction.
- α ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 4} is a coarse grid; large α may destabilise early training.
- lr = 2e-3 may destabilise training; that would be reported, not dropped.
- Grokking time resolution is 100 epochs.

## Amendment 1 (2026-11-22, before any Stage 5 run)

Added after reading the Stage 5 literature report (`LITERATURE_REPORT.md`, agent commit `7ec86fe`)
and before launching. It changes no code, run grid, decision rule or frozen prediction.

**H4 gains a directional prediction.** He et al. (arXiv:2602.16849) prove, for gradient flow from
small initialisation, that each neuron's winning frequency is fixed by its initial spectral magnitude
and phase. They do not address the train/test split.
*Prediction:* the median key-frequency Jaccard for pairs that share the init seed (different split)
is higher than for pairs that share the split seed (different init). Reported descriptively from the
existing H4 output. Their setting (MLP, small-initialisation limit) differs from ours, so a failure
would be informative rather than a contradiction. Grokking-time decomposition stays exploratory.

**H1 context.** E2 largely replicates published delay laws. Truong et al. (arXiv:2605.18845; and
arXiv:2603.13331, see the citation note below) and Pracher et al. (arXiv:2609.26679) give a delay
that scales as 1/(lr·wd) under AdamW, up to constants. Those laws describe the delay *after*
memorisation, T_grok − T_mem, whereas H1 fits total T_grok. Memorisation time is not expected to
scale with lr·wd, so b_lr and b_wd are expected slightly shallower than −1, as in Stage 4B (−0.964).
The decision rule is unchanged.
*Secondary, exploratory:* repeat the H1 fit on T_grok − T_mem, with T_mem = first logged epoch
where train accuracy ≥ 0.99.

**H2 context.** The direction (slower with larger α) is consistent with Lyu et al.'s log α term and
with the log-norm-ratio term of Truong et al., through a larger weight norm at memorisation.

**H3 context.** Pracher et al. report critical slowing near an upper weight-decay cutoff. If the
1/wd law fails at wd = 3 or 5, the expected direction is *slower* than predicted.

**Citation note.** The arXiv listing for 2603.13331 is titled "Why Grokking Takes So Long: A
First-Principles Theory of Representational Phase Transitions"; the literature report gives a
different title. Equation and theorem numbers marked unverified in the report remain unverified.
All of this is to be checked against the full papers before anything is cited publicly.

## Deviations

Amendment 1 adds one prediction and literature context before any run existed; it changes no

### Deviation 1 (2026-11-22, after the H0 check, before H1–H5 were interpreted)

**H0 failed.** Seeds 0, 2 and 3 grokked at 5,700, 10,200 and 4,800 epochs against Stage 4B's
5,500, 9,500 and 5,500. Seeds 1 and 4 were consistent. Diagnosis:

- **Same starting point.** Epoch-0 training loss for seed 3 is identical to 15 significant digits.
- **Same code.** `model.py`, `data.py`, `train.py` and the tests are unchanged between the Stage 4B
  commit (`0fd723a`) and Stage 5 (`a8fb8c2`). The `sweep.py` additions reproduce the original bit for bit
  on CPU, and logging every 100 vs 500 epochs gives bit-identical training on CPU.
- **Same environment.** torch 2.15.0+cu132 and NVIDIA driver 595.99.02 in both. Only the Linux
  kernel, and the driver's kernel-module build for it, changed between the two runs.
- **The GPU trajectories already differ by epoch 500** (seed 3 train loss 8.270e-4 vs 8.227e-4).

**Conclusion.** GPU arithmetic was not reproducible between these two runs. Small rounding
differences shift grokking time by about 200–700 epochs, roughly 4–13%. The H0 rule wrongly assumed
bit-exact reproduction across separate GPU runs. That was a design error in the check, not a fault in
the pipeline.

**Consequences.**
1. Comparisons *within* Stage 5 are unaffected: every Stage 5 run comes from one pipeline, and this
   noise is part of their random error.
2. Forecasts against the frozen Stage 4B predictions (the H1 forecast error, the H3 score) include this
   noise floor. Forecast errors below about 10% cannot be distinguished from it.
3. Stage 4B's recorded commit (`0fd723a`) does not contain `sweep.py`, which was committed after those
   runs. Stage 4B's sweep code is therefore pinned only by the later export.

**Follow-up after Stage 5.** Rerun one configuration twice on the GPU with identical settings, and once
with Stage 4B's 500-epoch logging, to establish whether GPU runs are reproducible given identical
settings.
